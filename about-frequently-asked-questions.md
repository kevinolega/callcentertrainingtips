# CALL CENTER JOBS AND CAREER ADVICE

### What should I do about my career?

Build your skills and slowly upgrade to a higher-paying job.

Look for opportunities to use your existing skills, gain experience, and qualify for better work.

### How do I become a call center agent?

Make sure you have the following qualities:

- You have good conversational English skills.
- You have a customer service-oriented mindset.
- You are confident at introducing yourself.
- You think like a problem solver.
- You ask questions when you don't know or fully understand what the other person is saying.
- You can give step-by-step instructions and tell stories that convince other people to cooperate with you.

Do the following steps:

1. Make a list of reputable call center companies in your location.
2. Organize the list of call centers and research their addresses and contact information.
3. Send your resume online, call their offices, and visit companies that accept walk-in applicants.
4. Aim to attend 16 interviews.
5. Expect some interviews to go badly and some job offers to be unsuitable.
6. Use each interview to improve your preparation and find better opportunities.

# ENGLISH COMMUNICATION AND TRAINING

### How do I improve my English?

Don't rely only on watching English movies.

Spend more time practicing the words, sentences, and conversations you need for your goals.

Start with our [free reviewer](https://forms.gle/sJDPEd9sD9Yf3g3J8).

You can also join our [group English training](https://callcentertrainingtips.com/group-sessions/) or [private coaching sessions](https://callcentertrainingtips.com/english-lessons/).

### How should I learn English?

Here's a simple guide:

1. Start by getting a notebook.
2. Write down your introductions, questions you want to ask, and questions you want to answer.
3. Take note of step-by-step instructions for all your skills.
4. Write down your personal and career stories.

Make it a habit to improve your writing by finding and correcting your mistakes.

Share your writing with friends and mentors and seek their feedback.

Practice speaking your answers aloud.

### Why is my progress slow?

I began realizing I had bad English when I was ten.

I spent years watching English movies, imitating people, and practicing conversations with friends.

At eighteen, I couldn't get hired in a call center, so I got a job in sales.

I started writing lessons and collecting techniques and skills in my notebook.

My first ten years of work were a mix of failures and a little success.

I kept writing, practicing, and trying to improve.

I've been playing this game every day, gaining experience and building my skills.

Keep practicing and be patient.

### How much practice do I need?

I recommend three levels of practice.

**LEVEL 1: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU START TO GET IT RIGHT.**

Learn the basics.

Study sample answers, practice sentences, and correct your mistakes.

Your goal is to start giving correct answers, even if you still need help.

**LEVEL 2: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU GET IT RIGHT CONSISTENTLY.**

Answer questions without scripts.

Practice explaining your ideas in your own words.

Correct mistakes that keep coming back.

Your goal is to repeat good performance instead of getting lucky once.

**LEVEL 3: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU CAN'T GET IT WRONG.**

Practice difficult questions, interruptions, unexpected situations, and realistic interviews.

Learn to recover when you forget an answer or make a mistake.

The goal isn't to memorize the perfect answer.

**The goal is to become someone who can handle the interview.**

### Why do you compare English practice to martial arts?

I practice Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu and other martial arts.

In boxing, you first learn your stance, footwork, and punches.

You practice through shadowboxing, bag drills, and cooperative partner exercises.

Then you progress to controlled sparring and opponents who actively resist.

Eventually, you test your skills under competition pressure.

English communication and job interviews follow a similar process.

First, you learn how to answer correctly.

Next, you practice until you can answer consistently.

Finally, you practice handling difficult and unexpected questions.

Knowing an answer is different from being able to deliver it under pressure.

# PERSONAL DEVELOPMENT AND CAREER QUESTIONS

### What should I do with my life?

Read "[Happy, Smart, and Useful](https://sivers.org/hsu)" by Derek Sivers.

### I can't focus. What should I do?

Focus is one of the most important habits you can develop.

The fear of being stuck in low-paying jobs pushed me to put more effort into developing my focus.

Here are some of the things I do:

- I make sure I get enough sleep.
- I quit smoking.
- I try to eat enough protein and take care of my diet.
- I exercise.
- I meditate. I used the Headspace app to learn how.
- I take a shower at the start of my day.
- I take a nap if I need a reset.
- I put my phone inside my backpack when I need to finish important work.

Sometimes, this means I take a long time to reply to messages.

Yes, it's hard.

Websites, games, apps, and media compete for our attention.

You need to protect your focus if you want to make progress.

### I have a boring, well-paying job. Should I quit to pursue art full-time?

Read "[How to Do What You Love and Make Good Money](https://sivers.org/balance)" by Derek Sivers.

### I'm a full-time artist but can't make money. What should I do?

I have a friend who worked in a call center while spending his free time making art.

Eventually, he built a reputation and earned good money from his side work.

You can build your skills and income from your art while keeping a job that pays your bills.

### What do you think of this business idea?

It doesn't matter what anyone thinks, except your customers.

Nobody can predict whether it will work.

Stop asking only theoretical questions.

Find one paying customer and please that person.

Start small and move up slowly as you become more successful.

### Where can I find a mentor?

First, read "[How to Ask Your Mentors for Help](https://callcentertrainingtips.com/mentor/)".

Then read "[A Warning to Anyone Receiving Advice](https://sivers.org/advice/)" by Derek Sivers.

Read books.

Meet people.

Talk to people who succeeded.

Talk to people who failed.

Learn from their experiences.
