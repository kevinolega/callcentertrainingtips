# SEVEN PROBLEMS THAT CAN COST BUSINESS OWNERS MONEY

## 1. YOU STRUGGLE TO SELL YOUR SERVICES.

A potential client asks about your service.

You explain what you do.

You list the features.

You talk about your experience.

Then the client asks:

"Why should I hire you?"

You give another long explanation.

The client says they'll think about it.

You never hear from them again.

Maybe you didn't understand their real problem.

Maybe you spent too much time talking about yourself.

Maybe you couldn't explain the results your service provides.

We'll practice how to:

- Ask better discovery questions.
- Identify the customer's problem.
- Explain your offer.
- Present your services.
- Discuss the value of your work.
- Answer customer objections.
- Ask for the sale.
- Follow up with interested customers.

**A sales conversation should help the customer understand why your service is worth buying.**

## 2. YOU KEEP LOWERING YOUR PRICES.

You know what your service is worth.

You know how much it costs to deliver.

But when the customer says your price is too high, you panic.

You offer a discount.

You include more work for free.

You lower your rate.

You make promises that your team will struggle to keep.

And after winning the client, you realize the project won't make enough money.

**You won the sale but lost part of your profit.**

We'll practice how to discuss prices without immediately offering discounts.

You'll learn how to explain your service, discuss budgets, respond to objections, and negotiate changes to the scope.

**You don't need every client. You need clients who understand and can pay for the value you provide.**

## 3. YOUR EMPLOYEES DON'T UNDERSTAND YOUR INSTRUCTIONS.

You explain what needs to be done.

Your employee says they understand.

A few days later, they submit the wrong work.

You get frustrated.

You repeat the instructions.

They make another mistake.

You start wondering why you hired them.

But what if your instructions weren't clear?

Maybe you didn't explain the expected result.

Maybe you left out important steps.

Maybe you never confirmed the deadline.

Maybe your employee didn't know which task had priority.

We'll practice how to give instructions that explain:

- What needs to be done.
- Why the work matters.
- What the finished result should look like.
- Who is responsible.
- When the work is due.
- What to do when something goes wrong.
- When to ask for help.

**Clear instructions reduce confusion and help employees do their jobs.**

## 4. YOU AVOID DIFFICULT CONVERSATIONS.

An employee keeps missing deadlines.

A client asks for work outside the agreement.

A customer demands a refund.

A business partner disagrees with your decision.

You know you need to discuss the problem.

But you keep putting it off.

Maybe you don't want an argument.

Maybe you're afraid the client will leave.

Maybe you struggle to explain your position in English.

Eventually, the problem becomes worse.

We'll practice:

- Giving negative feedback.
- Discussing missed deadlines.
- Handling complaints.
- Explaining business decisions.
- Responding to unreasonable requests.
- Discussing scope changes.
- Setting boundaries.
- Saying no professionally.

**Avoiding a difficult conversation doesn't make the problem disappear.**

## 5. YOU STRUGGLE TO LEAD MEETINGS.

You bring everyone together.

You explain what's happening.

People ask questions.

The conversation goes in different directions.

The meeting takes longer than expected.

But afterward, nobody knows exactly what to do.

We'll practice how to:

- Set a meeting agenda.
- Explain the purpose of a meeting.
- Ask useful questions.
- Present problems.
- Discuss options.
- Make decisions.
- Assign tasks.
- Confirm deadlines.
- Summarize the next steps.

**A meeting should help your team make decisions and move work forward.**

## 6. YOUR MARKETING DOESN'T EXPLAIN WHY PEOPLE SHOULD BUY.

You post on social media.

You run advertisements.

You update your website.

You tell people about your products and services.

But the messages don't generate enough inquiries.

Maybe you're focusing on features instead of customer problems.

Maybe your offer isn't clear.

Maybe your call to action doesn't explain what people should do.

We'll practice writing:

- Social media captions.
- Service descriptions.
- Promotional messages.
- Sales offers.
- Landing page copy.
- Follow-up messages.
- Customer replies.
- Business introductions.

**Your marketing needs to help people understand what you offer and why they should care.**

## 7. YOU STRUGGLE TO COMMUNICATE WITH INTERNATIONAL CLIENTS.

Your business can serve clients outside the Philippines.

But you struggle when it's time to speak with them.

You worry about your grammar.

You struggle with unfamiliar accents.

You don't know how to explain technical details.

You hesitate during negotiations.

We'll practice:

# SELL YOUR SERVICES WITHOUT IMMEDIATELY OFFERING A DISCOUNT.

Imagine a potential client asks:

"Why is your service more expensive than your competitor's?"

You could say:

"We provide good quality, and we have many years of experience."

That answer doesn't explain much.

Let's try a better response.

"Our service includes project planning, regular progress updates, quality checks, and a clear review process. These steps help us catch problems before the final delivery."

Now the client has something specific to compare.

You can explain what the client receives.

Why the process matters.

And what makes your offer different.

**We'll practice explaining the value of your service before discussing discounts.**

# STOP PAYING FOR UNCLEAR INSTRUCTIONS.

Imagine you assign a task to an employee.

You say:

"Please finish the client report by Friday."

On Friday, the employee submits a report.

But important information is missing.

The report needs another review.

You spend more time explaining what should have been included.

Now imagine giving a clearer instruction:

"Please finish the client report by Friday at noon. Include last month's campaign results, three problems we identified, and your recommendations for next month. Send the draft for review before sharing it with the client."

Now the employee knows what to prepare.

The deadline is clear.

The expected result is clear.

The review process is clear.

**Better communication can prevent problems before they become expensive.**
