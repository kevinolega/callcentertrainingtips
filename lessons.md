<h2>THREE LEVELS OF PRACTICE</h2>

<p>Reading lessons and watching videos can teach you what to say.</p>

<p>But knowing the correct answer isn't the same as being able to say it during a real interview.</p>

<p>That's why I recommend three levels of practice.</p>

<h3>LEVEL 1: START TO GET IT RIGHT</h3>

<p><strong>Practice until you start to get it right.</strong></p>

<p>Read the lessons, study sample answers, and practice basic English communication.</p>

<p>Learn how to introduce yourself, build sentences, pronounce common words, and answer interview questions.</p>

<p>You can use sample answers and scripts while learning.</p>

<p>Your goal is to start giving correct answers, even if you still make mistakes.</p>

<h3>LEVEL 2: GET IT RIGHT CONSISTENTLY</h3>

<p><strong>Practice until you get it right consistently.</strong></p>

<p>Answer questions without reading a script.</p>

<p>Explain your ideas in your own words.</p>

<p>Practice different versions of the same question and correct mistakes that keep coming back.</p>

<p>Your goal is to give good answers repeatedly, not just when you get an easy question.</p>

<h3>LEVEL 3: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU CAN'T GET IT WRONG</h3>

<p><strong>Practice until you can't get it wrong.</strong></p>

<p>Practice handling unexpected questions, interruptions, difficult follow-up questions, and realistic interviews.</p>

<p>Learn to recover when you forget your answer or make a mistake.</p>

<p>Your goal is to perform well even when things don't go according to plan.</p>

<p><strong>The goal isn't just to memorize the correct answers. The goal is to become someone who can handle the interview.</strong></p>
