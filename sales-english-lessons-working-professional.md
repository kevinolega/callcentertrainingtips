# SEVEN PROBLEMS THAT CAN KEEP YOUR CAREER STUCK

## 1. YOU DO GOOD WORK BUT DON'T GET RECOGNIZED

You complete your tasks.

You solve problems.

You help coworkers.

But when your manager asks about your achievements, you don't know how to explain them.

You say things like:

"I just do my job."

"I help the team."

"I finish my tasks."

Those answers don't explain what you contribute.

We'll practice describing your work using specific examples.

You'll learn how to explain:

- Problems you've solved.
- Projects you've completed.
- Improvements you've made.
- Responsibilities you've handled.
- Results you've helped achieve.
- Skills you've developed.

**You need to make it easier for managers to see the value you bring to the company.**

## 2. YOU STAY QUIET DURING MEETINGS

You attend meetings.

You listen to the discussion.

You may have an idea that could help.

But you don't speak.

You're afraid someone will disagree.

You're worried about your grammar.

You don't know how to interrupt politely.

Or you struggle to organize your thoughts.

Then someone else shares an idea similar to yours.

And they get the credit.

We'll practice:

- Asking questions.
- Sharing opinions.
- Explaining recommendations.
- Agreeing and disagreeing professionally.
- Speaking when you need clarification.
- Giving updates.
- Responding to unexpected questions.

**You don't need to talk more than everyone else. You need to know how to speak when your contribution matters.**

## 3. YOU STRUGGLE WITH DIFFICULT CONVERSATIONS

Sometimes, your manager gives you unfair criticism.

A coworker blames you for a mistake.

A client makes an unreasonable request.

Someone disagrees with your decision.

You don't know how to respond.

You become defensive.

Or you stay quiet even when you need to explain your side.

We'll practice handling:

- Negative feedback.
- Disagreements with coworkers.
- Difficult managers.
- Workplace misunderstandings.
- Missed deadlines.
- Unexpected requests.
- Conflict over responsibilities.
- Conversations about mistakes.

**You can disagree with someone and still communicate professionally.**

## 4. YOU DON'T KNOW HOW TO ASK FOR A RAISE

You've worked for the company for years.

You've gained experience.

You've taken on more responsibilities.

But your salary hasn't increased.

You want to ask for a raise.

Yet you don't know how to start the conversation.

You worry your manager will reject your request.

You may feel embarrassed talking about money.

We'll practice how to:

- Explain your contributions.
- Discuss your achievements.
- Present examples of your work.
- Ask about salary adjustments.
- Discuss additional responsibilities.
- Respond to objections.
- Negotiate compensation.
- Ask what you need to qualify for an increase.

**You need to learn how to discuss your value instead of hoping someone notices your effort.**

## 5. YOU KEEP GETTING PASSED OVER FOR PROMOTION

You've applied for promotion.

Maybe you've been with the company longer than the other candidates.

Maybe you know the processes better.

But another employee gets the position.

Sometimes, the problem isn't your technical skills.

You may struggle to explain your achievements.

You may have difficulty answering leadership questions.

You may not know how to discuss conflict, coaching, or decision-making.

We'll practice promotion interviews and professional conversations.

You'll learn how to explain why you're ready for more responsibility.

**Your next role requires you to communicate at the level of the job you want.**

## 6. YOUR EMAILS AND REPORTS DON'T EXPLAIN YOUR WORK CLEARLY

You know what happened.

You know what the problem is.

But writing the explanation takes too long.

Your emails become confusing.

Your reports include too much information.

Your manager asks more questions because the message isn't clear.

We'll practice:

- Writing professional emails.
- Giving project updates.
- Explaining problems.
- Writing incident reports.
- Summarizing meetings.
- Recommending solutions.
- Requesting approvals.
- Confirming next steps.

**Good writing helps people understand the problem and decide what to do next.**

## 7. YOU STRUGGLE DURING INTERVIEWS FOR BETTER JOBS

You want a higher-paying job.

You have relevant experience.

But when the interviewer asks about your achievements, you struggle to answer.

You forget important examples.

You use long, confusing explanations.

You don't know how to explain why you deserve a higher salary.

We'll practice:

- Introducing yourself.
- Explaining your career history.
- Discussing your achievements.
- Answering difficult interview questions.
- Explaining career changes.
- Discussing your strengths.
- Answering leadership questions.
- Talking about salary expectations.

**You need to learn how to explain why your experience makes you worth hiring.**

# THREE LEVELS OF PRACTICE

I use the same training principles in martial arts and professional communication.

In boxing, you first learn your stance, footwork, and basic punches.

You practice through shadowboxing and drills.

Then you train with partners who move and react.

Eventually, you test your skills against opponents who actively resist.

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu follows a similar process.

You learn techniques with a cooperative partner.

Then you practice with resistance.

Finally, you test your skills against someone who's trying to stop you.

Professional communication follows the same principle.

## LEVEL 1: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU START TO GET IT RIGHT

Learn the correct approach.

You may practice:

- Introducing yourself.
- Building clear sentences.
- Explaining your work.
- Writing professional emails.
- Giving short updates.
- Answering interview questions.
- Asking professional questions.

You can use sample answers, scripts, and guided exercises.

**Your goal is to start communicating correctly, even if you still need help.**

## LEVEL 2: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU GET IT RIGHT CONSISTENTLY

Practice without depending entirely on scripts.

You may practice:

- Explaining ideas in your own words.
- Speaking during meetings.
- Responding to follow-up questions.
- Writing clear reports.
- Explaining problems and solutions.
- Discussing your achievements.
- Handling routine workplace conversations.

Your goal is to communicate well across different situations.

## LEVEL 3: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU CAN'T GET IT WRONG

Now we add pressure.

We'll practice:

- Difficult promotion interviews.
- Challenging questions from managers.
- Salary negotiations.
- Workplace disagreements.
- Unexpected presentation questions.
- Negative feedback.
- Conversations about mistakes.
- Situations where someone challenges your ideas.

You'll practice responding without losing your confidence.

**Your goal is to communicate clearly even when the conversation becomes difficult.**

- Your achievements.
- Additional responsibilities.
- Problems you've solved.

- Skills you've developed.
- Salary expectations.
- Compensation negotiations.
- Career development goals.

"I've worked here for three years, so I deserve a raise."

"Over the past three years, I've taken responsibility for client reports, trained new employees, and improved how our team tracks deadlines. I'd like to discuss a salary adjustment that reflects my current responsibilities."

**The goal is to make your request clear and support it with your work.**
