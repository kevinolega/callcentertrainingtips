# SEVEN PROBLEMS WE CAN HELP YOU FIX

## 1. FAILING KPIs AND FEAR OF TERMINATION

Your company expects you to meet specific performance targets.

But your scores remain below target.

You receive another coaching session.

Another warning.

Another reminder that your numbers need to improve.

You try harder.

But your results stay the same.

**Working harder won't fix a problem you haven't identified.**

We'll review your performance concerns.

We'll identify the communication skills, processes, or work habits that may affect your scores.

Then we'll practice correcting them.

## 2. FAILING REGULARIZATION

You worked hard to pass the interviews.

You got hired.

But you're still on probation.

Now you need to prove that you can meet the company's standards.

Maybe your communication skills are holding you back.

Maybe you struggle with processes.

Maybe you understand the lessons during training but forget what to do during live calls.

We'll work on identifying your weak areas.

Then we'll practice the skills you need.

**The goal is to help you become the agent your company wants to keep.**

## 3. ANGRY, RUDE, AND RACIST CALLERS

Some customers are friendly and easy to help.

Others shout, insult you, or make racist comments.

They interrupt you.

They refuse to listen.

They blame you for problems you didn't cause.

Sometimes, one difficult call affects the rest of your shift.

We'll practice how to:

- Respond to angry customers.
- Handle personal insults and racist comments.
- Maintain professional boundaries.
- Ask questions when customers refuse to cooperate.
- Avoid arguments.
- Follow your company's escalation procedures.
- Recover after difficult calls.

**You can't control how customers behave. But you can learn how to handle difficult conversations without losing your confidence.**

## 4. DIFFICULT ENGLISH ACCENTS

You understand English when you watch familiar speakers.

But real customer calls are different.

Some customers speak too fast.

Others mumble.

Some have accents you aren't familiar with.

You ask them to repeat themselves.

They become impatient.

You panic because the call is taking too long.

We'll practice:

- Understanding different English accents.
- Recognizing common expressions.
- Asking customers to repeat information.
- Using clarification questions.
- Confirming names, numbers, and details.
- Summarizing what you understood.
- Recovering when you miss information.

**You don't need to understand every word immediately. You need to know what to do when you don't understand something.**

## 5. COMPLICATED PROCESSES

Sometimes, your biggest problem isn't English.

You don't know what to do next.

The customer has a complicated concern.

You don't know which procedure applies.

You struggle to find the right information.

You worry about giving the wrong answer.

We'll practice:

- Breaking complicated procedures into steps.
- Identifying the customer's actual problem.
- Asking better probing questions.
- Finding the information you need.
- Explaining procedures to customers.
- Recognizing when to ask for help.
- Following a clear problem-solving process.

**The goal is to understand the problem, follow the process, and explain the solution clearly.**

## 6. STRUGGLING WITH MULTIPLE TOOLS

Call center agents often need to use several systems during one call.

You may need to:

- Search customer records.
- Open several applications.
- Check account information.
- Follow troubleshooting procedures.
- Read knowledge base articles.
- Update tickets.
- Document the conversation.

All while the customer waits.

We'll practice how to organize your work and communication.

We'll use sample scenarios to help you manage conversations while following computer-based procedures.

**The goal is to reduce confusion and improve how you handle your work.**

## 7. DIFFICULTY EXPLAINING SOLUTIONS

You may understand the customer's problem.

You may even know the correct solution.

But you struggle to explain it.

Your sentences become too long.

You repeat yourself.

The customer gets confused.

The call takes longer.

We'll work on grammar, sentence structure, pronunciation, and clear explanations.

**Knowing the answer is different from knowing how to explain it.**

# THREE LEVELS OF PRACTICE

I use the same training principles in martial arts and workplace coaching.

In boxing, you start by learning your stance, footwork, and punches.

You practice through shadowboxing and bag drills.

Then you practice with partners who move and react.

Eventually, you test your skills against opponents who actively resist.

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu follows a similar process.

You first learn techniques with a cooperative partner.

Then you practice with controlled resistance.

Finally, you test your skills against opponents who are trying to stop you.

Call center work follows the same principle.

## LEVEL 1: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU START TO GET IT RIGHT

Learn the correct approach.

Practice:

- Asking the right questions.
- Following call flow.
- Explaining solutions.
- Using professional expressions.
- Understanding common accents.
- Following procedures.
- Handling basic customer concerns.

We'll use scripts, examples, and guided exercises when needed.

**Your goal is to start doing things correctly, even if you still need help.**

## LEVEL 2: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU GET IT RIGHT CONSISTENTLY

Practice different versions of the same customer problem.

Learn to explain solutions without depending entirely on scripts.

Correct mistakes that keep coming back.

Practice using tools while speaking with customers.

**Your goal is to perform well repeatedly, not just when you get an easy call.**

## LEVEL 3: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU CAN'T GET IT WRONG

Practice difficult situations.

These may include:

- Angry customers.
- Racist or abusive remarks.
- Unexpected questions.
- Complicated customer problems.
- Interruptions.
- Unfamiliar accents.
- Multiple tasks during a call.
- Situations where you don't know the answer.
- Customers who refuse to cooperate.

You'll practice responding, recovering from mistakes, and following the correct process.

**Your goal is to maintain good performance even when the situation becomes difficult.**
