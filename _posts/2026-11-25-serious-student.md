The kind of students I want to help and the kind of community I want to build.

I've been helping people prepare for call center jobs since 2005.

I've shared free lessons, videos, interview tips, English exercises, and training resources.

Many people have used my free resources to get hired without paying me for training.

I've also worked with students who needed more personal help.

Over the years, I've encountered different kinds of students.

Some started with very poor English but eventually got hired. Others had better English skills but struggled to make progress.

The difference often comes down to their habits, decisions, and actions.

Before we discuss what it means to be a serious student, let's look at some examples.

## Good and Bad Examples of Student Behavior

### Example 1: Using Free Resources

**Bad example:**

A student asks for help. I send them the free reviewer. They don't read it.

A few days later, they return and ask the same questions that the reviewer already answers.

**Good example:**

A student downloads the free reviewer, studies the lessons, watches the videos, and takes notes.

They return with questions about details I did not cover.

### Example 2: Watching Training Videos

**Bad example:**

A student watches a few videos and expects their English to improve.

They spend more time looking for new videos than practicing the lessons.

**Good example:**

A student watches all the training videos.

They watch the important videos multiple times.

They take notes, complete the exercises, and practice the examples.

### Example 3: Reading Website Lessons

**Bad example:**

A student opens a lesson, reads the first few sentences, and asks me to explain everything through chat.

**Good example:**

A student goes through all the website lessons.

They read the important lessons multiple times.

They write down what they learned and prepare questions about things they still don't understand.

### Example 4: Asking Questions

**Bad example:**

"Sir, how do I pass a call center interview?"

The student has not studied the available lessons or attempted to prepare their answers.

**Good example:**

"Sir, I watched your self-introduction videos and read your interview guide. I wrote three versions of my introduction. Which version should I use if I have no previous work experience?"

The student has already done some work.

I have something specific to review.

### Example 5: Applying for Jobs

**Bad example:**

A student applies to two companies, gets rejected, and concludes that no company will hire them.

**Good example:**

A student submits more than 30 online job applications and attends more than 10 interviews.

They collect interview questions, review their mistakes, and continue improving their answers.

Every interview gives them more information about what they need to improve.

### Example 6: Taking Notes

**Bad example:**

A student attends a training session without taking notes.

During the next session, they ask me to explain the same lesson again.

**Good example:**

A student takes notes during every session.

They review their notes before the next session.

They return with new questions, examples, and improved answers.

### Example 7: Receiving Feedback

**Bad example:**

A student sends me their interview answer.

I provide corrections.

They never revise their answer.

Later, they ask me for more interview tips.

**Good example:**

A student sends me their interview answer.

I provide corrections.

They revise their answer, practice it, and send me an updated version.

They also tell me which parts they still find difficult.

### Example 8: Sending Updates

**Bad example:**

A student asks for advice.

I spend time helping them.

They disappear.

Weeks later, they return with another problem. I have no idea what happened to the previous advice I gave them.

**Good example:**

A student asks for advice, applies it, and sends me an update.

They tell me what they tried, what worked, what did not work, and what happened during their interview.

Even if they fail, their update helps me understand what we need to work on next.

### Example 9: Having Limited Money

**Bad example:**

A student tells me they cannot afford paid training.

I give them access to free resources.

They ignore the resources and continue asking for personal coaching.

**Good example:**

A student tells me they cannot afford paid training.

They study the free lessons, take notes, practice, and apply for jobs.

They make good use of everything available to them.

### Example 10: Paying for Training

**Bad example:**

A student pays for training but does not practice between sessions.

They expect their English to improve simply because they paid and attended classes.

**Good example:**

A student pays for training, attends sessions, takes notes, completes exercises, practices between sessions, and applies the feedback they receive.

They make the most of the money they invested.

### Example 11: Dealing With Rejection

**Bad example:**

A student gets rejected and spends the next few weeks complaining about recruiters, interviewers, and company requirements.

They make no changes to their preparation.

**Good example:**

A student gets rejected.

They write down the interview questions they remember.

They review their answers, identify their mistakes, practice, and apply to another company.

### Example 12: Getting Hired

**Bad example:**

A student receives help throughout their job application process.

They get hired but never send an update.

**Good example:**

A student sends me an update when they get hired.

They share what helped them, what they learned, and what other applicants should know.

Their experience becomes useful information for the next group of students.

### Example 13: Participating in the Community

**Bad example:**

A student only visits the community when they need something.

They ignore discussions, practice opportunities, and questions from other students.

**Good example:**

A student participates in discussions, shares job openings, practices with other students, and answers questions when they know the answer.

They help create the kind of community they want to be part of.

## Which Kind of Student Are You?

You may recognize some of your own habits in the bad examples.

You can change those habits.

You can start taking notes today.

You can start reading the lessons today.

You can start practicing today.

You can submit another job application today.

You can send a progress update today.

You do not need to wait until your English improves.

You do not need to wait until you have money for training.

You do not need to wait until you feel confident.

Becoming a serious student starts with your actions.

## The Kind of Students I Want to Help

Over the years, I've noticed certain habits among students who make progress.

They study.

They take notes.

They practice.

They ask questions.

They apply for jobs.

They learn from their mistakes.

They keep me updated.

These are the kinds of students I want to help.

You don't need to speak perfect English.

You don't need to be smart, confident, or talented.

You don't even need to have money for paid training.

However, you need to take responsibility for your own improvement.

I want you to understand two levels of student development.

**Level 1: Serious Beginner**

Someone who has started developing good study and practice habits.

**Level 2: Serious Student**

Someone who has built a record of study, practice, action, and follow-through.

Your goal is to move from a serious beginner to a serious student.

## Level 1: Serious Beginner

**Starting point**

A serious beginner may have limited knowledge, skills, and experience. However, they are ready to learn and take action.

You can become a serious beginner today.

You do not need to prove that you are already good at something.

You need to start developing the habits that will help you become good at it.

### Study Habits

A serious beginner:

- Reads the instructions before asking questions.
- Downloads the free call center reviewer.
- Starts reading the website lessons.
- Starts watching the training videos.
- Prepares a notebook or digital document for notes.
- Writes down important lessons.
- Makes time to study and practice.
- Reviews lessons they find difficult.
- Searches for answers before asking for help.

### Practice Habits

A serious beginner:

- Attempts the exercises.
- Writes their own interview answers.
- Practices their self-introduction.
- Reads English sentences out loud.
- Records themselves speaking English.
- Identifies words they find difficult to pronounce.
- Accepts that mistakes are part of learning.
- Makes corrections based on feedback.
- Tries again when they get something wrong.

### Job Application Habits

A serious beginner:

- Prepares or updates their resume.
- Searches for available call center jobs.
- Starts submitting online job applications.
- Accepts interview invitations.
- Attends scheduled interviews.
- Writes down interview questions.
- Keeps a record of their applications.
- Continues applying after rejection.

### Communication Habits

A serious beginner:

- Asks clear and specific questions.
- Explains what they have already tried.
- Shares their difficulties.
- Communicates when they need help.
- Sends updates about their progress.
- Respects the time of the people helping them.
- Takes responsibility for their next steps.

You don't need to have completed everything on this list.

You need to start.

## Level 2: Serious Student

**Target level**

A serious student has developed a record of consistent effort.

They have moved beyond expressing interest.

They can show what they have studied, practiced, attempted, and learned.

They have developed habits that allow them to continue improving even when nobody tells them what to do.

### 1. Studies All Available Resources

A serious student:

- Has watched all the relevant training videos.
- Has watched the important videos multiple times.
- Has gone through all the relevant website lessons.
- Has read the important lessons multiple times.
- Has gone through all the free reviewer resources.
- Has completed the relevant exercises.
- Has reviewed lessons related to their weaknesses.
- Returns to previous lessons when they encounter problems.

They understand that watching a video once does not mean they have mastered the lesson.

Some lessons will make more sense after you attend your first interview.

Other lessons will make more sense after you experience rejection.

You may discover important details when you watch a video for the second or third time.

Reviewing old lessons is an important part of your education.

### 2. Takes and Reviews Notes

A serious student:

- Takes notes while watching videos.
- Takes notes while reading lessons.
- Takes notes during training sessions.
- Writes down useful examples.
- Records important corrections.
- Keeps a list of unfamiliar words.
- Writes down questions they want to ask.
- Reviews their notes regularly.
- Uses their notes when preparing for interviews.

They do not depend entirely on their memory.

They also do not expect their teacher to repeat every lesson whenever they forget something.

Your notes become your personal reviewer.

Over time, your notes should contain information about your specific strengths, weaknesses, mistakes, and improvements.

You can use a notebook, a document on your computer, or a notes app on your phone.

The tool does not matter as much as the habit.

### 3. Asks Intelligent Questions

A serious student asks questions that demonstrate effort and understanding.

They ask about details that the lessons did not cover.

They ask how to apply a lesson to their specific situation.

They ask for help when they encounter a problem they cannot solve on their own.

Here are some examples.

**General question:**

How do I pass a call center interview?

**Better question:**

I watched your self-introduction video and wrote my own answer. I have no work experience. What other experiences can I include in my introduction?

**General question:**

How do I improve my English?

**Better question:**

I practiced the sentence patterns in your English lessons. I can write my answers, but I have difficulty answering without looking at my notes. How should I practice?

**General question:**

Why do I keep failing interviews?

**Better question:**

I've attended 12 interviews. I usually pass the initial interview but fail the final interview. These are the questions I remember. Can you help me identify what I need to improve?

**General question:**

Can you give me interview answers?

**Better question:**

I wrote answers to the interview questions in your reviewer. I practiced them for three days. Here are the questions I still have difficulty answering. Can you help me improve these answers?

You don't need advanced English to ask intelligent questions.

You need to study, think, and explain your situation.

A good question tells me:

- What you want to accomplish.
- What you already know.
- What you have already tried.
- What happened when you tried it.
- What you still need to understand.

The more information you provide, the easier it is for me to help you.

### 4. Practices Regularly

A serious student:

- Writes their own interview answers.
- Revises their answers after receiving feedback.
- Practices speaking English regularly.
- Practices answering questions without reading a script.
- Records and listens to their own voice.
- Identifies mistakes in their speech.
- Works on grammar and pronunciation.
- Practices mock interviews.
- Practices mock calls.
- Repeats difficult exercises.
- Measures their improvement over time.

They understand that knowledge and skill are different.

Reading about swimming does not teach you how to swim.

Watching interview videos does not automatically make you good at interviews.

Reading English lessons does not automatically make you good at speaking English.

You need practice.

You need to give yourself opportunities to make mistakes, identify those mistakes, and correct them.

### 5. Takes Real-World Action

For students who are still looking for a call center job, these are important milestones:

- Has submitted more than 30 online job applications.
- Has attended more than 10 job interviews.
- Has explored opportunities with different companies.
- Has experienced different interview questions.
- Has attempted different assessments.
- Has kept a record of application results.
- Has identified common reasons for rejection.
- Has made changes based on previous experiences.
- Continues applying while improving their skills.

These numbers are milestones, not requirements you must complete before you deserve help.

If you get hired before reaching these numbers, congratulations. You have achieved your goal.

However, if you are still unemployed, do not give up after a few applications or interviews.

Give yourself more opportunities to succeed.

Every application gives you another chance to receive an interview invitation.

Every interview gives you another chance to get hired.

Every rejection gives you an opportunity to review your preparation and identify what you need to improve.

You don't need to wait until your English is perfect before applying.

You can study, practice, and apply for jobs at the same time.

### 6. Accepts and Applies Feedback

A serious student:

- Listens when someone points out a mistake.
- Asks questions when they do not understand a correction.
- Takes notes about the feedback.
- Attempts the recommended solution.
- Revises their work.
- Submits the improved version.
- Reports the results.
- Continues practicing until they improve.

You do not need to agree with every piece of advice.

However, you should be prepared to test reasonable advice before dismissing it.

Feedback becomes useful when you apply it.

If I correct your grammar, practice the corrected sentence.

If I recommend a change to your interview answer, write a new version.

If I recommend a speaking exercise, attempt the exercise.

If I recommend that you apply to more companies, submit more applications.

Then tell me what happened.

This allows us to determine whether the advice worked or whether we need to try something else.

### 7. Sends Regular Updates

A serious student keeps the people helping them informed.

They send updates about:

- Lessons they have completed.
- Videos they have watched.
- Exercises they have attempted.
- Job applications they have submitted.
- Interviews they have attended.
- Questions they encountered.
- Assessments they passed or failed.
- Problems they are trying to solve.
- Advice they have applied.
- Improvements they have noticed.
- Job offers they have received.
- Their progress after getting hired.

They share both good news and bad news.

You do not need to wait until you succeed before sending an update.

Your failures can help us identify what you need to work on next.

When you send updates, I can understand your situation better.

I can identify patterns in your mistakes.

I can recommend more specific lessons.

I can avoid giving you advice that you have already tried.

I can help you decide what to work on next.

You should not expect the people helping you to keep asking whether you have studied, practiced, applied for jobs, or attended interviews.

Take the initiative to send an update.

### 8. Takes Responsibility for Their Progress

A serious student:

- Does not wait for someone to remind them to study.
- Does not expect someone else to complete their exercises.
- Does not expect a teacher to apply for jobs on their behalf.
- Makes time for their education.
- Uses the resources available to them.
- Finds ways to practice despite limited resources.
- Learns from mistakes.
- Keeps their commitments.
- Continues working even when their teacher is unavailable.
- Understands that improvement requires effort.

A teacher can provide instructions, examples, corrections, and guidance.

The student still needs to do the work.

I can help you prepare for your interview.

I cannot attend your interview for you.

I can teach you how to improve your English.

I cannot practice English on your behalf.

I can give you information about job opportunities.

I cannot submit every job application for you.

Your education, career, and future are your responsibility.

### 9. Contributes to the Community

A serious student understands that other people are also trying to improve their lives.

When possible, they:

- Share useful job openings.
- Share interview experiences.
- Share helpful resources.
- Answer questions they know how to answer.
- Encourage beginners.
- Participate in practice sessions.
- Share lessons from their mistakes.
- Celebrate other people's progress.
- Respect students who are less experienced.
- Share their success stories.
- Help maintain a community where people can study, practice, and improve.

You do not need to be an expert to help someone.

Sometimes, sharing what happened during your interview can help another student prepare for theirs.

A student who has attended five interviews can share useful information with someone who has never attended an interview.

A student who recently got hired can help someone prepare for the same application process.

A student who has learned an important English lesson can help another student understand it.

You can contribute while you are still learning.

## How to Send a Progress Update

You can use this simple format when sending me an update.

**Lessons I completed:**

List the lessons and videos you studied.

**What I learned:**

Share the important lessons you remember.

**What I practiced:**

Describe the exercises or activities you completed.

**Job applications:**

How many applications have you submitted?

**Interviews:**

How many interviews have you attended? What happened?

**Problems:**

What are you having difficulty with?

**Questions:**

What specific questions do you want to ask?

**Next steps:**

What do you plan to work on next?

Your update does not need to be long.

It does not need to have perfect grammar.

It needs to give me enough information to understand your situation.

### Example of a Good Progress Update

Hi Sir,

I finished reading the free reviewer and watched your self-introduction and mock interview videos.

I watched the self-introduction video three times and wrote notes about the important parts.

I prepared my own introduction and practiced speaking for 30 minutes every day this week.

I submitted 15 online job applications this week. My total is now 34 applications.

I attended three interviews this week. My total is now 11 interviews.

I passed two initial interviews but failed one final interview.

I noticed that I have difficulty answering questions about my previous work experience.

I wrote down the questions I remember and prepared new answers.

Can you help me check whether my new answers provide enough details?

My plan is to practice these answers and submit another 10 applications this week.

Thank you.

### Why This Is a Good Update

The student tells me what they studied.

They tell me what they practiced.

They provide numbers.

They explain what happened during their interviews.

They identify a specific problem.

They have already attempted a solution.

They ask a specific question.

They have a plan for what to do next.

I have enough information to help this student without asking several additional questions.

## Do I Need to Pay to Become a Serious Student?

No.

You can become a serious student using the free resources on this website.

Many people have used my free lessons, videos, and reviewer to prepare for interviews and get hired.

Paid training is available for people who want more structure, personal feedback, speaking practice, and help with their specific problems.

Paying for training gives you access to additional services.

Your effort determines how much you learn from those services.

You can be a serious student without paying for training.

You can also pay for training and make very little progress if you do not participate.

Whether you choose free resources or paid training, you still need to study, practice, apply what you learn, and take action.

## What If I Have Limited Time or Money?

Start with what you have.

Use the free resources.

Study when you have time.

Take notes.

Practice.

Apply for jobs.

Ask specific questions.

Send updates.

Some students can study every day.

Others have jobs, children, family responsibilities, or limited internet access.

Work with the time and resources available to you.

If you have 30 minutes, use those 30 minutes.

If you can only study a few times a week, make good use of those opportunities.

You can make progress without having perfect conditions.

## What If My English Is Very Poor?

You can still become a serious beginner.

Your current English skills do not determine whether you are a serious student.

Your actions do.

You can start with simple sentences.

You can practice basic grammar.

You can learn new words.

You can read lessons out loud.

You can write short answers.

You can make mistakes and correct them.

You can ask questions using the English you already know.

You do not need to feel embarrassed about your current skill level.

The purpose of studying is to improve your skills.

If you already knew everything, you would not need the lessons.

## What If I Keep Failing Interviews?

Keep a record of your interviews.

Write down the questions you remember.

Review your answers.

Identify the parts you found difficult.

Return to the relevant lessons.

Prepare better answers.

Practice those answers.

Apply to more companies.

Send updates about your progress.

If you have already attended more than 10 interviews, you have collected useful experience.

Use that experience to improve your preparation.

Do not allow your previous interviews to go to waste.

## The Difference Between an Audience Member, a Customer, a Student, and a Community Member

These groups can overlap, but they have different roles.

### Audience Member

An audience member watches videos, reads articles, follows social media accounts, and uses free resources.

There is nothing wrong with being an audience member.

The free resources are available to everyone.

### Customer

A customer purchases training, coaching, or another service.

I provide the services included in their purchase.

The customer decides how much effort to invest in their education.

### Serious Student

A serious student studies, practices, takes notes, asks questions, applies feedback, and sends updates.

They have developed a record of effort.

They can be a paying customer or someone who uses only free resources.

### Community Member

A community member participates in an environment where people study, work, and improve together.

They share useful information.

They help others when they can.

They contribute to the kind of community they want to be part of.

Someone can become a valuable student and community member without purchasing anything.

Someone can also purchase training without developing serious study habits.
