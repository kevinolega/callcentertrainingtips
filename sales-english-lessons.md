<h2>THREE LEVELS OF PRACTICE</h2>

<p><strong>Practice until you start to get it right.</strong></p>

<p><strong>Practice until you get it right consistently.</strong></p>

<p><strong>Practice until you can't get it wrong.</strong></p>

<p>I use the same training principles in martial arts and English communication.</p>

<p>In boxing, you begin with your stance, footwork, and basic punches.</p>

<p>You practice through shadowboxing, heavy bag drills, and partner exercises.</p>

<p>As your skills improve, you work with partners who move, react, and eventually fight back.</p>

<p>You progress through technical sparring, harder sparring, and competition.</p>

<p>Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu follows a similar process.</p>

<p>You learn techniques against a cooperative partner.</p>

<p>Then you practice with controlled resistance.</p>

<p>Eventually, you test those techniques against opponents who are actively trying to stop you.</p>

<p>The same principle applies to job interviews.</p>

<h3>LEVEL 1: START TO GET IT RIGHT</h3>

<p><strong>Learn the basics and begin performing them correctly.</strong></p>

<p>During private coaching, we'll identify your problems and begin correcting them.</p>

<p>You may practice:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Introducing yourself.</li>
  <li>Answering common interview questions.</li>
  <li>Building correct English sentences.</li>
  <li>Pronouncing common words and phrases.</li>
  <li>Explaining your work experience.</li>
  <li>Organizing your thoughts before speaking.</li>
</ul>

<p>You can use sample answers, scripts, and guided exercises.</p>

<p>I'll correct your mistakes and explain what needs to change.</p>

<p>You will make mistakes while learning.</p>

<p><strong>Your goal is to start getting things right, even if you still need help.</strong></p>

<h3>LEVEL 2: GET IT RIGHT CONSISTENTLY</h3>

<p><strong>Repeat good performance without depending on memorized answers.</strong></p>

<p>We'll reduce your dependence on scripts and practice more realistic conversations.</p>

<p>You may practice:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Answering interview questions in your own words.</li>
  <li>Explaining ideas without reading.</li>
  <li>Responding to follow-up questions.</li>
  <li>Correcting recurring grammar mistakes.</li>
  <li>Speaking clearly during longer conversations.</li>
  <li>Answering different versions of the same question.</li>
  <li>Recovering when you forget part of your answer.</li>
</ul>

<p>I'll identify mistakes that keep coming back and assign exercises to correct them.</p>

<p><strong>Your goal is to give good answers repeatedly, not just when you get an easy question.</strong></p>

<h3>LEVEL 3: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU CAN'T GET IT WRONG</h3>

<p><strong>Perform under pressure, even when things don't go according to plan.</strong></p>

<p>In boxing, you face active resistance through sparring and competition.</p>

<p>In BJJ, you test your skills against opponents who are trying to escape, control, or submit you.</p>

<p>In interview preparation, we add pressure through:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Realistic mock interviews.</li>
  <li>Unexpected questions.</li>
  <li>Difficult follow-up questions.</li>
  <li>Interruptions.</li>
  <li>Questions you don't fully understand.</li>
  <li>Situations where you forget your prepared answer.</li>
  <li>Conversations that don't follow a script.</li>
  <li>Practice while nervous or under pressure.</li>
</ul>

<p>Real interviewers won't always ask questions you've prepared for.</p>

<p>You need to think, respond, and recover when something goes wrong.</p>

<p><strong>Your goal is to handle the interview even when things don't go your way.</strong></p>
