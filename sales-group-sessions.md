# THREE LEVELS OF PRACTICE

**Practice until you start to get it right.**

**Practice until you get it right consistently.**

**Practice until you can't get it wrong.**

I use the same training principles in martial arts and English communication.

In boxing, you first learn your stance, footwork, and basic punches.

You practice through shadowboxing, punching bag drills, and cooperative partner exercises.

As you improve, you practice with a partner who moves, reacts, and eventually fights back.

You progress through technical sparring, harder sparring, and competition.

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu follows a similar process.

You first learn techniques with a cooperative partner.

Then you practice with controlled resistance.

Eventually, you test your techniques against opponents who are actively trying to stop you.

The same principle applies to job interviews.

## LEVEL 1: START TO GET IT RIGHT

**Learn the basics and begin performing them correctly.**

In boxing, you learn your stance, footwork, and punches.

You practice through shadowboxing, heavy bag drills, and cooperative partner exercises.

In BJJ, you learn movements, positions, and techniques with a cooperative partner.

In call center training, you practice:

- Introducing yourself.
- Answering common interview questions.
- Building correct English sentences.
- Pronouncing common words and phrases.
- Explaining your work experience.
- Organizing your thoughts.
- Using customer service expressions.

You can use sample answers, scripts, and guided exercises.

You will make mistakes.

That's part of learning.

**Your goal is to start giving correct answers, even if you still need help.**

## LEVEL 2: GET IT RIGHT CONSISTENTLY

**Repeat good performance without depending on scripts.**

In boxing, you practice combinations on the heavy bag, speed bag, and focus mitts.

You work with partners who move, react, and create openings.

You begin controlled technical sparring.

In BJJ, you practice positional drills, flow rolling, and controlled resistance.

In call center training, you practice:

- Answering questions without reading.
- Explaining your ideas in your own words.
- Responding to follow-up questions.
- Correcting recurring grammar mistakes.
- Speaking clearly during longer conversations.
- Handling different versions of the same question.
- Recovering when you forget part of your answer.

You won't always receive the same question.

You need to understand what you're saying instead of memorizing a script.

**Your goal is to give good answers repeatedly, not just when you get an easy question.**

## LEVEL 3: PRACTICE UNTIL YOU CAN'T GET IT WRONG

**Perform under pressure, even when things don't go according to plan.**

In boxing, you face active resistance through sparring and competition.

Your opponent isn't there to help you practice.

They're trying to land punches while preventing you from landing yours.

In BJJ, you roll against resisting opponents who are trying to escape, control, or submit you.

For call center interviews, we add pressure through:

- Unexpected interview questions.
- Difficult follow-up questions.
- Interruptions.
- Questions you don't fully understand.
- Realistic mock interviews.
- Situations where you forget your prepared answer.
- Conversations that don't follow a script.
- Practicing while nervous or under pressure.

A real interviewer won't always ask questions you've prepared for.

You need to think, respond, and recover when something goes wrong.

**Your goal is to handle the interview even when things don't go your way.**
